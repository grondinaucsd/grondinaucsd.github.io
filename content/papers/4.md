##### Abstract

We investigate the impact of sustainable investing—the investing practice that accounts for environmental/societal/governance factors—in a macroeconomic model with dynamic general equilibrium forces. Preference-driven sustainable investing impacts firms’ cost of capital and investors’ saving incentives, resulting in a dynamic feedback loop between productive capital reallocation and investor wealth ownership. We investigate the magnitude and timing of the impact in a calibrated quantitative model where sustainable preferences are disciplined by estimated portfolio return differentials. We finally present a policy implementation exercise that highlights the potential and the limits of sustainable investing as a market-driven mechanism to deliver policy-relevant macroeconomic impact.

*Notes:* Both panels use the empirical distribution and the baseline calibration targeting an initial portfolio return differential of 20 bp. Initial capital is at the all-Benchmark stationary allocation, and Advocate’s initial wealth share is 33%. Panel (a) reports Advocate’s wealth share on the left axis and percentage growth in aggregate capital, ΔKₜ, and capital at the highest-scoring group accounting for 25% of initial capital, ΔKₜ²⁵, on the right axis. Each capital series is measured relative to its own initial level, and group membership remains fixed throughout the transition. Panel (b) shows percentage capital growth per production unit relative to the all-Benchmark allocation. The horizontal axis measures cumulative initial capital share, ordered from lowest to highest ESG score. The solid marked line traces the ownership threshold gₜ*; the dashed line marks the boundary of the highest-scoring group accounting for 25% of initial capital.
